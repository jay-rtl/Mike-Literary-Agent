Have another question? ↗
What does a literary agent do?

A literary agent represents an author’s professional interests and may assist with positioning, submissions, negotiations, publishing agreements, rights, and longer-term opportunities.

Can published and unpublished authors inquire?

Yes. Projects can be considered at different stages. Existing publication agreements and available rights must be reviewed before determining what opportunities remain.

Can self-published authors seek representation?

Yes. Self-publication does not automatically prevent representation. Contracts, ISBN ownership, distribution arrangements, sales history, and rights status may need to be considered.

Does representation guarantee a publishing contract?

No. Representation provides strategy, advocacy, and submissions. Publishing decisions remain with publishers and acquisitions teams.

What are subsidiary rights?

Rights beyond primary publication, including translation, international editions, audio, film, television, and certain licensing opportunities. Availability depends on existing agreements.

Could my book be considered for film or television?

Potentially. Suitability depends on the material, available rights, audience, and adaptation potential. Representation does not guarantee a movie or series; producers, studios, financiers, and networks make their own decisions.
